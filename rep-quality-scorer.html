Target Tempo (seconds)
Concentric raises the stack, eccentric lowers it — told apart by direction, not by which came first. Pauses are judged both ways. Every rep is scored against the target and the scores averaged, not the other way round, so a rushed rep cannot be cancelled by a slow one: four reps at 4s and four alternating 2s and 6s share a mean and no longer share a score. A stroke is judged on the fast side always, and on the slow side only where a slow allowance is set — by default the concentric is free to run long and the eccentric is not. How much either phase may be off before it costs marks is set per phase under Phase allowance below. A turnaround with no pause at all is scored as the longest hold that could have gone unseen, not skipped.
Phase allowance (%)
How far off target a phase may be before it starts losing marks, as a percentage of that phase's target. This is the fast side for strokes and both sides for pauses. Lower the number on a phase to hold it closer.
Slow side — strokes only, and off unless a number is set. Blank means running long costs nothing, which is the right answer for the concentric: a client grinding out a long rep has earned the time, not lost control of it. A lowering that runs long is the opposite, so it is given a slow side by default.
Phase weighting
How much each phase counts toward the set score. Equal by default. A weight of 0 drops a phase from the score entirely — use it on a machine where a hold is not prescribed. These change the score only; the phase times themselves are measured the same way whatever you put here.

The whole set is analysed when you press Stop, which lets it filter without lag and see each turnaround in context. Gravity and sensor noise are measured from your own recording — no per-machine setup.

Peak accel needs to be at least 3× threshold. Below that, phase times read short rather than reps being missed outright, which is the one failure you cannot spot by eye — so it is called out above the score. Acceleration goes as stroke ÷ time², so halving the tempo costs four times the signal: stroke in mm ≳ 10 × (phase seconds)² on a firm mount, roughly 16× on a loose one. At a 4 s tempo that is about 160 mm firm, 250 mm loose. Mount rigidity moves this more than anything else here. A turnaround held for more than 0.25 s is seen; below that the end of one stroke and the start of the next are the same push, and the hold is scored at 0.25 s — the longest it could have been and still gone unmeasured.
